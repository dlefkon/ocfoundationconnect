Not all cracks are equal

Vertical cracks are the most common. Many are simple shrinkage cracks from the concrete curing, and a dry, hairline vertical crack that hasn't changed in years is often more nuisance than emergency. Horizontal cracks are different: they usually mean pressure from the soil outside is bending the wall — that's structural. Stair-step cracks through block or mortar joints often point to uneven settling.

The cracks that deserve a prompt look: any crack that leaks water, is wider than a pencil line, is growing, runs horizontal, or comes with a wall that's starting to bow or floors that are starting to slope.

How leaking cracks are usually fixed

For a non-structural leaking crack in poured concrete, the standard repair is crack injection — the crack is sealed and filled from the inside with a material that bonds through the wall and stops the water path. It's a targeted fix for a targeted problem. If the crack is a symptom of movement — bowing, settling, pressure — the specialist will tell you that sealing it alone won't hold, and explain what will.
