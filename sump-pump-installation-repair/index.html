The hardest-working appliance in the basement

A sump pump sits in a pit at the lowest point of your basement, collects the water your drainage system gathers, and pumps it outside — away from the foundation. When it works, you never think about it. When it fails during a storm, a basement can take on water fast.

Signs a pump is on its way out

  • It runs constantly, or cycles on and off rapidly
  • It makes grinding, rattling, or humming noises without moving water
  • The pit fills and the pump doesn't kick on (float or switch trouble)
  • It's more than about 7–10 years old and has never been serviced
  • It stopped during a power outage and the basement paid for it — a battery backup pump is the usual answer to that one

Installation, replacement, and backup

A specialist can replace a failed or undersized pump, add a battery backup so a storm outage doesn't flood the basement, or install a pump and pit where a waterproofing system needs one. If your pump just failed and there's water coming in, see what to do when a sump pump fails first.
