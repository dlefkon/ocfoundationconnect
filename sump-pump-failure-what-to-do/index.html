Sump pump failure: what to do

The pump quit mid-storm and the water is rising. Do these things first — then fix it so the next storm goes differently.

While it's failing

  1. Mind the electricity. Don't stand in water to reach plugs or the panel. If water is near outlets or appliances, treat it as an electrical hazard first.
  2. Check the simple things (from dry footing): is the pump plugged in? Did the breaker or GFCI trip? Is the float switch stuck against the pit wall or tangled?
  3. Check the discharge: is the outside line blocked, frozen, or disconnected — pumping water right back against the house?
  4. Bail or borrow time: a utility pump or a wet/dry vacuum can hold the line while you get help; move valuables and storage up off the floor.

Why pumps fail at the worst moment

Pumps fail during storms because storms are when they work hardest — and when power flickers. The common causes: a worn-out switch (the part that fails most), a pump at the end of its roughly 7–10 year life, a clogged intake, or an outage with no battery backup. If yours failed in the last storm, the pattern will repeat in the next one.

A specialist can replace the pump, fix the discharge, and — the upgrade that prevents the repeat — add a battery backup pump that keeps working when the power doesn't.
