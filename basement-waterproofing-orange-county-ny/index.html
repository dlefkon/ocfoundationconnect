Why Orange County basements get wet

Most wet basements here aren't a mystery. The county gets heavy spring rain on top of snowmelt, summer downpours, and fall nor'easters — and a lot of that water ends up against foundation walls. Homes near the Wallkill River, Moodna Creek, and the low ground along the Hudson and Delaware corridors deal with high water tables on top of it. When the soil around your foundation is saturated, water finds the easiest way in: floor-wall joints, cracks, and porous block.

What waterproofing actually involves

Real basement waterproofing is usually an interior drainage system: a channel installed along the inside perimeter of the foundation that collects water before it spreads across the floor and routes it to a sump pump, which sends it outside and away from the house. Exterior grading, gutters, and downspout extensions matter too — a good contractor will tell you if part of your problem can be solved from outside before selling you a system.

When to act

Water that shows up once after a freak storm is worth watching. Water that shows up every spring, puddles that keep coming back to the same corner, efflorescence (white crust) on the walls, or a sump pit that runs constantly — that's a pattern, and patterns get worse, not better. A free estimate costs you an hour and tells you which one you're dealing with.
