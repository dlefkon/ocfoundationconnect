Foundation problems don't heal

A foundation crack, a bowing wall, a floor that's started to slope — these are the house telling you something changed underneath it. In Orange County, the usual drivers are water and soil: heavy spring rain and snowmelt saturate the ground, clay-heavy soils in parts of the county swell and push, and freeze-thaw cycles work cracks wider year after year.

The housing stock here spans a wide range. Homes in Newburgh, Middletown, and Port Jervis often stand on stone or concrete-block foundations that are a century old. Post-war homes in New Windsor, Monroe, and the Middletown suburbs are block or poured concrete. The repair approach is different for each — which is why a real fix starts with someone looking at your actual foundation, not a phone quote.

What a foundation specialist does

Depending on what they find, a foundation contractor may seal and inject cracks, stabilize a bowing wall with anchors or reinforcement, or support a settling section of the house. The right method depends on your foundation type, the soil, and how far the problem has gone. That's the contractor's call after an in-person inspection — and the inspection and estimate are free through this service.
