Basement water after rain

The storm passed and left water on your basement floor. Here's what that pattern usually means — and what fixes it.

First, right now

  • Stay out of standing water if it may be in contact with electrical outlets, appliances, or your panel. Water and electricity together are the real emergency — if in doubt, call an electrician or your utility first.
  • Move what's salvageable up and off the floor; take photos for your records.
  • Check whether your sump pump is running, plugged in, and discharging outside.

Why a dry basement floods after a storm

The pattern is familiar across Orange County: the basement is fine for months, then a heavy rain — a summer thunderstorm cell, a nor'easter, the spring thaw — and there's water on the floor. The usual causes:

  • Hydrostatic pressure: saturated soil pushes groundwater up through the floor-wall joint and floor cracks. This is the #1 cause of "it only happens in big rain" basements.
  • Surface water: clogged gutters, downspouts dumping at the foundation, or soil graded toward the house sending roof water straight down the wall.
  • A crack or joint that's opened up — water takes the path it found last time, and widens it.
  • Sump pump failure or an overwhelmed pump — see what to do when a sump pump fails.

Which one you have decides the fix — and the price. Surface-water problems are sometimes solved with gutters and grading. Hydrostatic pressure usually calls for an interior drainage system. An honest contractor will tell you which problem you actually have before selling you anything.
