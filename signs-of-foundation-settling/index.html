Signs of foundation settling

Sticking doors, sloping floors, cracks that keep growing — how to tell normal aging from a foundation that's still moving.

The classic settling checklist

  • Doors and windows that suddenly stick, or won't latch
  • Cracks in drywall, especially diagonal ones running from door and window corners
  • Floors that slope, or gaps opening between the floor and the baseboards
  • Stair-step cracks in exterior brick or block, and cracks in the foundation itself
  • A chimney or porch pulling away from the house
  • Nails popping and trim separating in rooms that were fine before

Settling vs. a house just being old

Almost every house settles a little in its first years, and old houses in Newburgh, Middletown, and Port Jervis have had a century to find their level — a hairline crack that hasn't moved in a decade is usually just history. Settling that matters is uneven and ongoing: one corner dropping, cracks that keep growing, doors that get worse season by season. The pattern to respect is change.

Orange County's mix of soils — including clay that swells when wet and shrinks in dry spells — can move a foundation a little every year. A specialist can measure whether your house is still moving and, if it is, explain the support options. The inspection and estimate are free.
